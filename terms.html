1. What Halal Table is, and what it is not

Halal Table lists food businesses and shows what the community knows about whether they are halal. Every listing carries one of four trust labels:

  • Certified — a moderator has seen a certificate from a named certifying body and recorded it.
  • Community confirmed — at least three more members have confirmed the place is halal than have disputed it, each saying how they know (they ate there, saw a certificate, were told by staff, and so on).
  • Unverified — listed, but nobody has confirmed it yet. Most listings start here.
  • Disputed — members disagree about whether the place is halal.

We are not a halal certifier. We do not inspect kitchens, audit suppliers or verify slaughter. Labels reflect documents we have been shown and what members report, both of which can be wrong or out of date. A business can change owners, suppliers or menus at any time. Whether food is halal enough for you is a decision only you can make. Ask the business, look for their certificate, and use your own judgement. We accept no responsibility for that decision.
